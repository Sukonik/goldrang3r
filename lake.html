Four breaches, one morning

Exploitation, confirmed

Hackers are now actively breaching Linux systems through the OpenSSH “ssHole” authentication bypass, CVE-2026-123158, using it to run commands, steal credentials and set up persistent access — days after the fix shipped, with SANS honeypots already logging attempts from 13 IP addresses. The same morning, CISA added a second actively exploited flaw to its catalog: CVE-2026-102895, an authentication bypass in GPUStack that gives unauthenticated attackers full administrative access, including creating persistent admin API keys, with a federal patch deadline of November 12. Both flaws share a shape — authentication that can simply be walked around — and both are already being used, not just theorized.

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

The tools that bite back

Fake GitHub security tools have infected more than 2,000 PCs with malware that disables defenses and steals data, Doctor Web found — a campaign that ran for two years, turning the very tools people download to stay safe into the attack. On the same day, a flaw in the APT package manager used by Debian and Ubuntu, CVE-2026-109518, was disclosed: under certain conditions it can be exploited to install a package and run code as root. The lesson of both is the same — the software you trust to protect or update your system is itself a target, and the fix in both cases is to get it from a source you can verify.

  • Cybernoz: Fake GitHub security tools infected 2,000+ PCs with malware that disables defenses and steals data, Doctor Web finds — a campaign running for two years
  • The Hacker News: Debian and Ubuntu APT flaw CVE-2026-109518 lets an attacker run code as root when installing a package under certain conditions

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

A bad morning for crypto custody

A flaw in Web3Auth let an attacker drain Keplr crypto wallets set up with Google or Apple sign-in, stealing at least $1 million, per GoPlus — and Web3Auth responded by emptying other at-risk wallets into recovery itself. Separately, Ankr's Ethereum bridge lost more than $303,000 after an attacker replayed a redeemed deposit receipt to bypass withdrawal checks in five unauthorized withdrawals. And California ordered crypto ATM operator CoinFlip to pay more than $4.8 million to fraud victims, mostly elderly, after its roughly 500 ATMs failed identity checks since 2021. Three different weak points — the sign-in layer, the bridge, and the ATM — and the same outcome: money gone before anyone noticed.

  • The Hacker News: A Web3Auth flaw let an attacker drain Keplr crypto wallets set up with Google or Apple sign-in — at least $1M stolen, per GoPlus; Web3Auth emptied other at-risk wallets into recovery
  • DeFi Planet: Ankr's Ethereum bridge loses $303K+ after an attacker replays a redeemed deposit receipt to bypass withdrawal checks in five unauthorized withdrawals
  • PANews: California orders crypto ATM operator CoinFlip to pay $4.8M+ to fraud victims — mostly elderly — after its ~500 ATMs failed identity checks since 2021

Two regulators, two breach stories

The Philippines' Bureau of Internal Revenue is probing an alleged breach of more than 10 million taxpayer records, flagged by monitoring group Deep Web Konek and attributed to a threat actor calling itself Happy Go Lucky PH — but the agency has not confirmed a breach happened, and the dataset's authenticity is unverified. Cluster · Morning edition · Summary — Tech Security

Exploitation outruns the patch

Zyxel's GS1900 switches were being exploited through a zero-day, CVE-2026-107441, from around August 17 — and the June patch everyone blamed the attacks on never covered it, because the exploit abused a previously unknown flaw rather than the June bug. Researchers count about 996 switches hit across 48 countries, and more than half still had factory-default credentials. The same pattern shows up in the week's other headline flaw: SANS honeypots are already recording live exploitation attempts for the OpenSSH 'ssHole' authentication bypass, CVE-2026-123158, days after the November 5 fix, with sensor evidence from November 7. In both cases the fix exists — and the attackers got there first.

  • AI Agent Update: Zyxel's June patch never stopped the GS1900 attacks — a zero-day, CVE-2026-107441, was exploited from mid-August against about 996 switches in 48 countries
  • SecurityTabs: SANS honeypots are seeing live exploitation attempts for the OpenSSH 'ssHole' auth bypass, CVE-2026-123158, days after the fix — alongside SonicWall's exploited MFA bypass and a cloud-fixed Entra privilege bug

Cluster · Morning edition · Summary — Tech

Agents everywhere, trust still catching up

DeepSeek's AI-agent platform has reached a reported 1.54 million daily users, and Mira Murati's Thinking Machines has released Inkling 2 Small, the highest-scoring open-weight model outside China — the agent era is scaling fast, and the models are getting smaller and cheaper. But the institutions are pulling back at the same time: Goldman Sachs is restricting Claude Code and GitHub Copilot to its roughly 12,000 engineers, cutting off the bankers and traders who had been using the agents to track client flows and build materials, days after a report found Claude Code's settings file had leaked secrets into nearly 2,000 public GitHub repos. Adoption and caution are arriving in the same week, from opposite directions.

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

K-entertainment's second breach in a week

SM Entertainment says an external attacker broke into its internal business systems around November 4 and copied stored documents — including employee names, phone numbers, addresses, bank account numbers, resident registration numbers, and contract details — before the company blocked the attack paths and rotated the compromised access keys. The breach also swept through affiliates DearU, which runs the fan-messaging app Bubble, and concert producer Dream Maker, though SM says no fan data on Bubble was touched. It is the second major Korean entertainment breach in six days, after HYBE disclosed its own internal leak on November 2, and the pattern is the same both times: the attackers went for the approval and document systems, not the customer-facing platforms. SM has not said how many people are affected, and says no secondary misuse has been confirmed so far.

  • Seoul Economic Daily: SM Entertainment discloses a Nov 4 breach that leaked employee resident registration numbers, bank account numbers and contract details across SM, DearU and Dream Maker — six days after HYBE's leak

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

The patch that will never come

SonicWall has disclosed two flaws in its Global Management System, the console that manages fleets of its firewalls — and says neither will ever be patched, because the product has reached end of life. The worse of the two, CVE-2026-109593, scores 9.1 and lets an unauthenticated attacker with network access read internal GMS files; the second, CVE-2026-109594, is a SQL injection rated 6.8 that needs high privileges but can read or change database records. Both were reported by researchers at Pentest-Tools, and SonicWall's only advice is to migrate to its replacement, Network Security Manager. There is no reported exploitation yet, but the vendor's own track record argues against waiting: CISA has added 19 SonicWall flaws to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog in four years, 13 of them also used by ransomware gangs.

  • BleepingComputer: SonicWall warns two flaws in end-of-life Global Management System will never be patched — CVE-2026-109593, a 9.1 unauthenticated file read, and a 6.8 SQL injection — and tells customers to migrate

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

The laundering trail moves to bitcoin

The Binance hackers have started swapping stolen XRP into bitcoin, Elliptic told reporters, with about $50 million moved mostly through THORChain, a service that shifts assets between blockchains — a move that puts the money beyond the reach of stablecoin issuers that can freeze tokens. The laundering trail now spans more than 20,000 wallets across 16 blockchains and at least 65,000 transactions, with roughly $3.1 billion still sitting in addresses linked to the theft. The shift is also sharpening a policy fight: the stalled CLARITY Act draft lacks hot-wallet limits, insurance, and customer protections that might have limited the damage, and the CFTC chair is using the breach to argue for federal oversight of offshore exchanges. Binance says it will cover the loss and has published reserves to show it can.

  • The Frontier: Elliptic tracks Binance hackers swapping about $50M in stolen XRP into bitcoin via THORChain as roughly $3.1B stays in linked wallets, sharpening calls for exchange security standards in crypto legislation

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

Contained — except it wasn't

Mercado Bitcoin's attacker kept emptying customer deposit addresses for about four hours after the exchange said the breach was contained, according to blockchain data from Bitquery, which traced $48.6 million taken across ten blockchains — above the $40 million first reported. The same attacker took about $391,000 from wallets Bitquery ties to BitMart, whose founder confirmed a November 2 hack and suspects the same group, with the BitMart transfers starting roughly 53 minutes before the first Mercado Bitcoin funds moved. Mercado Bitcoin says no customer funds were lost and about $11.2 million has been frozen with help from Tether, Circle and others, but deposits and withdrawals remain suspended with no reopening date. The two hits, hours apart and sharing an attacker address, look less like isolated incidents than one operation working down a list.

  • CoinDesk: Mercado Bitcoin's attacker kept emptying customer deposit addresses for four hours after the exchange said the breach was contained, Bitquery finds — and the same group hit BitMart for about $391K the same day

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

The fourth Mirae affiliate falls

Mirae Asset Securities, South Korea's largest broker, has confirmed a November 4–5 hack that leaked account numbers for about 3,800 customers and personal data of 35,255 current and former staff — days after regulators unveiled new countermeasures for the sector. A follow-up report puts the total at about 39,000 records and names Mirae Asset Securities as the third Korean brokerage hit, after Daishin and Hanyang, and the fourth Mirae Asset affiliate breached, after its life insurance, global investments and energy infrastructure arms. The attackers hit a mobile-web phone-authentication login service and the group's groupware a day apart, and the company says no resident registration numbers, passport numbers or financial damage have been confirmed so far. The timing is the story: the breach landed the same week the crackdown was announced, and the sector's defenses are being tested faster than they are being built.
