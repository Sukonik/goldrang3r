Tuesday, November 3, 2026

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

A million TV boxes, one built-in backdoor

Google and its partners have taken apart a botnet of more than 1.3 million Android TV boxes, and the uncomfortable part is how it got there: the report says the manufacturer built a backdoor into the firmware itself, then sold access to criminal affiliates who turned living-room hardware into attack infrastructure. Banning the maker from certified Android devices closes one door, but the boxes already in homes are the problem that lingers, since a device bought for streaming should never have been a foothold in the first place. If a cheap TV box seems too good a deal, this is why its software supply chain matters more than its price.

  • BleepingComputer: Google and partners dismantle the XCPE botnet of more than 1.3M Android TV boxes, saying maker Videostrong deliberately built in a firmware backdoor and sold access to criminal affiliates — and ban the firm from certified Android devices

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

Gorbag's harvest, in the open

A Russia-aligned group researchers call Gorbag has been quietly siphoning credentials, VPN configurations, and documents from Ukrainian courts, police departments, and front-line city councils since February 2025, with its latest intrusions riding a path-traversal flaw in WinRAR. The targets are telling: local government offices hold the everyday paperwork of a country at war, and VPN configs are exactly the keys that turn one stolen file into a way back inside a network. Naming the group does not stop it, but it gives every similar office a concrete checklist — patch the archiver, rotate the VPN credentials, and assume anything the group touched is already copied.

  • The Hacker News: ESET unmasks Gorbag, a Russia-aligned group harvesting credentials, VPN configs and documents from Ukrainian courts, police and front-line city councils since February 2025, lately via a WinRAR path-traversal flaw

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Tech Security

The AI campaign that keeps finding new victims

Blockchain infrastructure provider Chainstack has disclosed that an attacker reached a development environment on October 26 and escalated into parts of its production systems, exposing customer account details and password hashes, plus node credentials it is treating as exposed even without evidence they were read. The company links the intrusion to a large-scale, AI-powered attack campaign that surfaced last week, the same wave that previously hit Chainalysis and the API platform WorkOS — a pattern where one autonomous toolkit probes many targets and the victims only connect the dots afterward. The practical takeaway is unglamorous and urgent: rotate credentials now, because a hash in someone else's hands is a countdown, not a maybe.

  • The Hacker News: Chainstack discloses a breach of production systems exposing customer data and password hashes, assessed as part of a large-scale AI-powered attack campaign

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

Two payment tools, two very different fixes

Two payment products show the two ends of the patching spectrum this week. BTCPay Server says attackers exploited a flaw fixed in version 2.4.2 to drain bitcoin and litecoin from hot wallets on servers running 2.4.1 or earlier, and it is urging operators onto the latest 2.4.7 release — a fix that exists, if operators actually install it. The CryptoPayment Gateway plugin for WordPress has no such escape hatch: a maximum-severity flaw in versions 1.2.1 through 1.2.2 lets unauthenticated attackers delete files and pull wallet credentials in cleartext, the plugin is already closed to new downloads, and exploitation attempts are already being observed. One story is a reminder to update; the other is a reminder to remove what cannot be updated.

  • The Hacker News: BTCPay Server says attackers exploited a flaw fixed in 2.4.2 to drain bitcoin and litecoin from hot wallets on servers running 2.4.1 or earlier, and urges operators to install the latest 2.4.7 release
  • The Hacker News: Attackers target an unpatched maximum-severity flaw in the CryptoPayment Gateway WordPress plugin that can expose wallet credentials

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

The heist, from a fake interview to the vault

Binance's own account of the $6.3 billion theft reads less like a smash-and-grab and more like a slow compromise: it began in June, when a wallet-team employee downloaded a tampered coding tool after a bogus job interview, and by October 20 the intruders had used that foothold plus an internal AI tool to take over wallet developers' systems. On October 29 they triggered the drain, abusing an internal wallet-signing service and Ethereum's EIP-7702 delegation, while about $2.85 billion has since been frozen or intercepted. The lesson for every firm with a wallet team is that the interview, the download, and the developer laptop are now front-line security controls — the vault door held, but the keys were copied months earlier.

  • Blockchain.News: Binance's initial findings on its October 29 incident point to unauthorized access to an internal wallet-signing service and abuse of Ethereum's EIP-7702 delegation, with about $2.85B frozen or intercepted
  • Reuters: Binance says hackers used a bogus job interview and a tampered AI tool to compromise a wallet developer's computer before the $6.3B theft

Cluster · Afternoon summaries · Summary — Fintech Security

Hot wallets drain, cold wallets get the push

Brazil's Mercado Bitcoin is the week's second exchange breach after Binance, with Chainalysis putting the loss at about $49 million across 17 chains and nearly $10 million frozen, while the exchange counts roughly $38 million in assets involved and says customers will not bear losses. Across the Pacific, South Korea's financial regulator responded to the Binance heist by convening an emergency meeting and pressing local exchanges to raise cold-wallet shares, add incident-response staff, and set aside extra reserves. Together the two stories sketch the new normal for exchanges: hot wallets are where the money moves, cold wallets are where it survives, and regulators are done waiting for firms to rebalance on their own.

  • CryptoNews: Chainalysis puts the Mercado Bitcoin breach at about $49M across 17 chains, with nearly $10M frozen, while the exchange counts about $38M in assets involved and says customers will not suffer losses
  • CoinLab: South Korea's FSC holds an emergency meeting after the $6.3B Binance hack and asks local exchanges to raise cold-wallet shares, add incident-response staff and set aside extra reserves
