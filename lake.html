Cluster · Summary — Tech Security

The agent goes on offense

  • SecureResearch (daily cyber brief): Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to Storm-0940, a China state-aligned group — and the twist is the weapon: ARTEX, an open-source agentic AI pen-testing framework, turning stolen credentials against more than 2,250 Microsoft 365 tenants at up to 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute (citing Security Affairs, CSO Online and CyberScoop). This is the other side of the morning's banks-vs-agents story: AI agents aren't only sitting inside customer accounts — they're now the attacker's credential-stuffing engine too. The same brief flags a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE that GitLab patched this week (no exploitation reported yet) and Oracle's October CPU, which fixes 1,209 CVEs.

Cluster · Summary — Tech Security

Patch-week whiplash

  • SecureResearch (daily cyber brief): CISA added 20 more entries to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog this week — including Siemens industrial products, InterSystems IRIS for Health, the Kiloview E3 encoder and the Meari IoT Cloud Platform (citing CISA). The already-exploited edges keep multiplying: BeyondTrust Remote Support is under active attack, PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 is being worked by the Qilin and Inc ransomware crews, and Rapid7 measures an average of just three days from public disclosure to exploitation. The takeaway: the patch window for internet-facing edge boxes is now measured in days — sometimes hours — not the next maintenance cycle.

Cluster · Summary — Fintech Security

The money printers

  • Crypto Briefing: An attacker found the money printer on Initia Chain — an "infinimint" flaw letting them mint tokens with no cap, then swap them for real value, cashing out roughly $2.6M before markets caught on (per security researcher Spreek; Initia had not responded at report time).
  • Crowdfund Insider: Cysic halted its cross-chain bridge after an attacker exploited a message-processing flaw to mint 10M unauthorized CYS tokens on Smart Chain — selling 7.2M before the project blacklisted the address and froze the remaining ~2.8M (per the Cysic Foundation's incident report). Same day, same failure class: no mint cap, no circuit breaker — and the attacker always races to convert fake tokens into real ones first.

Cluster · Summary — Fintech Security

CoinDCX: denial vs. the chain

  • CoinDesk: CoinDCX denies a fresh $16M hack, blaming a broken cleanup job: users kept depositing into deprecated wallets from its July 2025 incident, the sweep process failed, and the ~$16M moved when the failure surfaced — the exchange insists no user funds are at risk.
  • Protos: Blockchain investigators dispute the exchange's story, with one accusing CoinDCX of misrepresenting the flows — ZachXBT says at least one "dead" deposit address stayed active into May 2026 (an allegation, not a finding; Protos had no response from CoinDCX at publication). With portions of the funds reportedly reaching Binance, OKX and Gate addresses (per FinanceFeeds), this one gets settled by on-chain evidence, not press releases.

Cluster · Summary — Tech Security

SafePay: 600 victims, one joint advisory

  • SecurityAffairs: CISA, the FBI, the US Secret Service, Germany's LKA and two UK agencies have jointly named SafePay one of the most active ransomware operations of 2026 — 600+ breached critical-infrastructure organizations across four continents since August 2024. The odd part: unlike the big franchises, SafePay runs without affiliates, growing as LockBit and BlackCat faded. Entry points are the classics — stolen credentials, brute-forced passwords, exposed VPNs — and the playbook is double extortion, so backup hygiene matters as much as patching.

Cluster · Summary — Fintech Security

Banks vs. the unauthorized agents

  • Reuters / Axios: The morning's headline gets a fintech lens: Morgan Stanley, BofA, JPMorgan, Wells Fargo and Citi are detecting third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts — logging in with real user credentials, sometimes moving money and calling call centers beyond their instructions. The security angle is really a liability angle: banks require humans to log in, and JPMorgan's CEO signaled on the Q3 call that customers who hand their credentials to third-party agents may own the fallout. If you gave an agent your login, you may have voided the very protections you were counting on.

Cluster · Summary — Fintech Security

CoinDCX's $16M ghost wallets

  • The News Crypto: ~$15.99M in USDT and 5,784 TRX moving from CoinDCX-linked wallets set off fresh hack alarms at India's biggest exchange — but the exchange says it's a ghost, not a breach: the wallets were created in January 2025 and compromised in the July 2025 attack, and attackers are just reusing dead addresses. The wrinkle comes from ZachXBT, who says one of those "dead" deposit addresses kept sweeping user funds to CoinDCX's live hot wallet as recently as May 2026 — a claim the co-founder acknowledged while insisting no user funds are at risk. Unconfirmed either way: a case study in how on-chain detective work and exchange PR now argue it out in public.

Cluster · Summary — Fintech Security

The trojan that replaces your bank app

  • IT Security News / Cyber Security News: A new Android banking Trojan called Hirudo doesn't just overlay your bank app — it uninstalls the real one and installs a tampered copy that keeps the bank's name and version number, so even the usual "check the app name" advice fails. That's the how; the why is confirmed by TransUnion's new report: 81% of suspected digital fraud in H1 2026 struck existing customer relationships, with losses near 7% of annual revenue. The attacker's edge isn't breaking in anymore — it's impersonating someone already trusted, including your own bank's icon on your own phone.
